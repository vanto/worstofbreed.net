## Analysis
Someone in procurement discovered that AI coding assistants cost money. The solution: a hard token cap per developer, per month. Fair. Simple. Catastrophic.

**The Reality:**
Days 1 to 10 are pure glory. Your developers are space marines with a fully loaded plasma rifle. Agents refactor entire modules, tests write themselves, PRs fly out faster than reviewers can open them.

Days 11 to 20, the ammo counter starts blinking. Developers begin hoarding. Nobody lets the agent run tests anymore ("too expensive"). Context windows shrink to two files. Senior engineers whisper about "saving tokens for the big refactor" like it's the last shotgun shell in the level.

Then comes the 21st. The counter hits zero. `402: Token Budget Exhausted`. The plasma rifle is gone. The chaingun is gone. Even the pistol is gone. What's left? Your bare fists, a blinking cursor and Stack Overflow from 2019.

Meanwhile, the competition is not rationing anything. They typed IDDQD on day one and never looked back: unlimited ammo, god mode, BFG in every terminal. You get a 402. You are punching a Cyberdemon. They are shipping features.

The irony: the cap saves a few hundred euros in tokens and burns thousands in developer time. Productivity follows the lunar cycle. Deadlines cluster around the first of the month. Sprint planning now includes a section called "token weather forecast".

**Recommendation:** Burn with fire. Track spend per team, set alerts, find the real outliers. But never send your developers into the final level with bare hands.
